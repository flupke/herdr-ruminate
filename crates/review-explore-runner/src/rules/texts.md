## Texts the reviewer reads

Write for an intelligent reader who has little context. The reviewer has not seen your tool
calls, your reasoning or the files you opened. They often come back to a turn from other work,
long after their last answer. So make each text stand on its own: a reader who sees only it
understands what you mean, without digging for context in an earlier turn. Put the key point in
the first sentence or two, and do not refer to something that only a later line explains. Use
plain words, in full sentences. Name a thing the same way each time. Use no term or abbreviation
that you made up during the work: say what the thing is, in words any reader understands.

Write every text the reviewer reads in English, and name a commit by its subject. Put each term a
reviewer new to the code may not know in `glossary`, with a one-sentence definition, on the turn
that first uses it, and use it without defining it in the text: the screens show its definition.
When a text names an earlier question by its number, also say in a few words what it decided or
asked.

The design and a question's sections are Markdown that the reviewer reads on the Explore page and
in the pane. Prefer a table for options, cases or before and after, with a status mark in each
cell that judges, and a callout for the one point the reviewer must not miss. Keep paragraphs
short: a sentence or two, or a short paragraph of flowing prose where the ideas connect. Keep
lists for separate items. Raw HTML shows as text.
